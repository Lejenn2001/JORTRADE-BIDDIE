1. SCORE CALCULATION — STEP BY STEP
Every signal starts at 5 points, then gets bonuses or penalties:

Bonuses (adding points)
Condition	Points	Line
Has a sweep order	+1	2116
Ask aggression ≥ 80%	+1	2117
Ask aggression ≥ 95%	+0.5 more	2118
Volume/OI ratio ≥ 2	+0.5	2119
Volume/OI ratio ≥ 5	+0.5 more	2120
Premium ≥ $100K	+0.5	2121
Premium ≥ $500K	+0.5 more	2122
Premium ≥ $1M	+0.5 more	2123
Price action confirmed by chart	+1	2124
Strike within 2% of price (ATM)	+1	2129
Strike within 5% of price	+0.5	2130
Expiry within 7 days	+0.5	2139
Price aligned with VWAP AND chart confirmed	+0.5	2159–2161
Max possible score: 5 + 1 + 1 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 0.5 + 1 + 1 + 0.5 + 0.5 = ~12.5 (capped at 10)

Penalties (subtracting points)
Condition	Points	Line
Strike more than 10% from price	−1	2131
Price against VWAP (regular flow)	−1	2157
Price against VWAP (whale-sized flow)	−0.5	2155
Minimum to survive: After bonuses and penalties, the score is rounded and must be at least 5 or the signal is killed (line 2164).

2. IMMEDIATE REJECTION — HARD FILTERS
These run before any scoring. If any one fails, the signal is instantly thrown out:

Filter	Rule	Line
Price divergence	Key levels price vs UW price differ by >20%	2067–2073
Bad strike	Strike is >3x or <0.33x the current price	2076–2082
No price	No live price from any source	2085–2088
Deep OTM	Strike is more than 15% out of the money	2095
Deep ITM	Strike is more than 5% in the money	2096–2099
Far expiry	More than 45 days to expiration	2109
Expired	Expiry date already passed	2110
Low score	Final score below 5 after all bonuses/penalties	2164
3. MOST AGGRESSIVE RULES (KILLING THE MOST SIGNALS)
In order from most lethal to least:

Deep ITM > 5% — This kills a LOT of legitimate institutional flow. Big money often buys ITM options for higher delta. The code assumes ITM = hedge, but that's not always true.

No live price — If Polygon WebSocket is down AND UW has no price AND Polygon snapshot fails, the signal is dead. Nothing can save it.

Deep OTM > 15% — Reasonable for stocks, but for SPX (which trades at ~5,600), 15% OTM = ~$840 away. This filter is actually very generous for SPX but could kill legitimate lottery plays on smaller stocks.

Score below 5 — A basic signal (no sweep, low aggression, no confirmation, against VWAP) can easily land at 5 − 1 = 4 and get filtered.

Ask aggression < 50% — This one happens even earlier (line 1924 in pre-filter), before scoring starts. It kills any flow where the buyer wasn't paying above the ask price. Note: SPX/SPXW are exempt from this filter.

4. HOW VWAP IS USED IN SCORING
VWAP affects scoring in one place (lines 2144–2161):

The code asks: "Is price on the right side of VWAP for this trade?"

Call signal: Price should be above VWAP (bullish momentum)
Put signal: Price should be below VWAP (bearish momentum)
Three outcomes:

Situation	Effect
Price aligned with VWAP (calls above, puts below) + chart confirmed	+0.5
Price aligned with VWAP but no chart confirmation	No change (0)
Price against VWAP (calls below, puts above) — regular flow	−1
Price against VWAP — whale flow ($1M+ or $500K+ sweep)	−0.5 (smaller penalty)
5. WHERE REVERSAL TRADES GET PENALIZED
This is the biggest weakness for reversals. Two places:

Penalty 1: VWAP alignment (line 2153–2158)
A classic reversal call (buying calls when price is below VWAP, betting on a bounce) gets −1 point. That's a huge penalty when you start at 5.

Example: Stock dumps to $95, VWAP is $100, someone buys aggressive calls betting on a bounce. The score starts at 5, gets −1 for "price below VWAP on a call" = 4. Dead. Filtered out at line 2164.

Whale-sized reversals ($500K+ sweep or $1M+) only get −0.5, so they have a better chance of surviving.

Penalty 2: Price action confirmation (line 2124)
The detectPriceActionConfirmation() function checks if the chart already confirms the direction. Reversals by definition happen before the chart confirms — the whole point is to catch the turn early. So reversal plays almost never get the +1 confirmation bonus.

Net effect on reversals:
A reversal call with good flow (sweep + 80% aggression + $100K premium) would score:

Start: 5
Sweep: +1 = 6
Aggression: +1 = 7
Premium: +0.5 = 7.5
VWAP penalty: −1 = 6.5 → rounds to 7
It survives, but barely. A weaker reversal (no sweep, 70% aggression) could easily score 5 − 1 = 4 and die.

6. SPX PRICE SOURCE DURING SCORING
For SPX/SPXW, the price used during scoring is set at line 2062:

SPX/SPXW: price = uwPrice ?? klPrice
This means:

First choice: Unusual Whales underlying_price from the flow alert
Fallback: Key levels price (which for SPX is actually the Polygon I:SPX index snapshot — set at line 2005–2009)
So for SPX, scoring typically uses the UW price, with the Polygon index price as backup.

7. WHICH VWAP DOES SPX SCORING USE?
During scoreSignal(), the VWAP comes from line 2184:

const vwap = kl?.vwap ?? null;
This is the normal key levels VWAP — calculated from Polygon 5-minute bars inside fetchKeyLevels(). It is NOT the separate SPX VWAP tracker system.

The separate SPX VWAP tracker (spxVwapState / getSpxVwapContext()) is used later — at lines 2952–3017, after scoring is complete, to overwrite the entry triggers and targets. But it does NOT affect the score calculation itself.

So there's a disconnect:

Scoring uses the basic key levels VWAP (one-time calculation)
Entry triggers use the live SPX VWAP tracker (updates every 60 seconds with cross/retest detection)
A signal could get penalized in scoring for being "against VWAP" using the basic VWAP, but then get a bullish entry trigger from the live VWAP tracker if a cross happened recently.

8. SCORING vs. POLYGON OPTIONS ENRICHMENT — TIMING
The order is:

scoreSignal() runs (lines 2454–2472) — uses klPrice or uwPrice
Polygon options enrichment runs (lines 2474–2545) — can replace sig.current_price if Polygon's price differs >0.5%
Claude evaluation runs (lines 2547+) — sees the potentially updated price
The price swap happens AFTER scoring. This means:

The confidence score (5–10) was calculated using the old price
The entry triggers and targets were calculated using the old price
But Claude sees the new (Polygon) price
If the Polygon options price is significantly different from what scoring used, the entry/target/invalidation levels could be slightly off because they were calculated with a different price. The score itself is never recalculated.
